# Business Story 01 · Bei ileile, chaguo jipya

**Owner review · 18 Nov 2026 · 47s Reel · Swanglish female host**

**Audience:** small product sellers and people designing a storefront. One takeaway: adding a meaningful option changes what the other options are compared with. It does not lower the price or make that option right for everyone. The example is juice, not an Altraware price list or a real offer.

| Example | Total TSh | TSh per 100 mL | When it can make sense |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 250 mL | 3,000 | 1,200 | Kujaribu · smaller spend and quantity |
| 500 mL | 5,000 | 1,000 | Mtu mmoja · more than a taste without buying a litre |
| 1 L | 9,000 | 900 | Kushare · lowest price per 100 mL here, but highest amount paid |

**Proof:** add the 1 L third tier; the same 500 mL at TSh 5,000 moves from most expensive of two to middle of three. `price-proof.json` and `example.json` record the rank and `price ÷ mL × 100` arithmetic. All examples trade quantity for price; no fake throwaway tier. Nothing here measures choice share, real preference, revenue, margin or a three-tier uplift.

**Concept source, checked 18 Nov 2026:** Itamar Simonson, [“Choice Based on Reasons: The Case of Attraction and Compromise Effects”](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/choice-based-reasons-case-attraction-compromise-effects), *Journal of Consumer Research* 16(2), 158–174, 1989, [DOI 10.1086/209205](https://doi.org/10.1086/209205). Stanford's abstract reports that alternatives can gain share when they become compromises in a choice set. The publisher page confirmed the article; full text was not accessed. Say **linaweza**, not “three always outperform two” and not “everyone picks the middle.” This example illustrates a compromise position, not an asymmetrically dominated decoy or a field study.

## English adaptation

Half a litre for TSh 5,000 starts as the higher-priced option. Add a one-litre bottle at TSh 9,000 and that exact same TSh 5,000 lands in the middle. That's the context shift behind the compromise effect, not a discount or a guarantee of sales.

Give each option a reason to exist. Show the quantity, total and comparable unit price together. Someone buying a taste might still want the smallest bottle; someone sharing might prefer the litre.

**Who is your third size for?** Prices are illustrative.
